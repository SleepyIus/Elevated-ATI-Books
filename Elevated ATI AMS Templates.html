Basic Concept - Ch 94

Moderate Sedation Readiness

A1AnesthesiaAirway first

Use this when a sedation question asks what must be ready before the procedure and what must be monitored until recovery.

Related Content

  • Moderate sedation keeps the client responsive enough to protect the airway while reducing pain and anxiety.
  • Common procedures include endoscopy, cardioversion, wound care, and minor procedures.

Underlying Principles

  • Older adults need closer monitoring because medication clearance can be reduced.
  • Airway, ventilation, blood pressure, level of consciousness, and capnography guide safety decisions.

Nursing Interventions

  • Verify consent, IV access, oxygen, suction, emergency equipment, and reversal agents.
  • Continue monitoring until presedation assessment findings return and discharge criteria are met.

Medication - Ch 94

Dantrolene for Malignant Hyperthermia

A7EmergencyRapid response

Use this when anesthesia suddenly triggers hypermetabolism, rigidity, rising carbon dioxide, dysrhythmias, or dangerous temperature elevation.

Purpose

  • Dantrolene is the priority medication used to stop the muscle-driven hypermetabolic crisis.
  • It belongs inside a coordinated malignant hyperthermia response, not as an isolated task.

Safety Monitoring

  • Stop the triggering anesthetic, support oxygenation, start cooling, and prepare intensive monitoring.
  • Trend vital signs, dysrhythmias, laboratory values, urine output, and organ-perfusion concerns.

Client Education

  • Teach the client and family that malignant hyperthermia risk is inherited and must be reported before future anesthesia.

Basic Concept - Ch 95

Preventing Surgical Complications

A1Preop safetyRisk reduction

Use this when the preoperative question asks which risks should be corrected or taught before the client reaches the operating room.

Related Content

  • Preop complications can stem from respiratory depression, fluid overload, infection, abnormal labs, medication effects, or thromboembolism risk.
  • Consent, medication review, skin/bowel preparation, and risk-factor screening are part of surgical readiness.

Underlying Principles

  • Uncontrolled blood glucose, elevated creatinine, or high WBCs can require provider notification before surgery.
  • Immobility and surgical stress increase clot risk, so prevention begins before the procedure.

Nursing Interventions

  • Clarify consent questions through the provider and witness only after the client understands.
  • Teach coughing, deep breathing, splinting, incentive spirometry, and ordered DVT prevention.

Nursing Skill - Ch 96

PACU Airway and Circulation Assessment

A9PACUABC priority

Use this when a newly arrived postoperative client needs immediate assessment after anesthesia handoff.

Indications

  • Every PACU transfer requires immediate full-body assessment with airway, breathing, and circulation first.
  • Aldrete scoring supports recovery readiness by tracking activity, consciousness, respiration, oxygen saturation, and circulation.

Steps

  • Assess airway patency, breath sounds, oxygenation, chest movement, vital-sign trends, bleeding, drains, and perfusion.
  • Position an unresponsive client laterally and keep rescue equipment available.

Report

  • Report worsening blood pressure trends, poor oxygenation, airway obstruction cues, excess bleeding, or urine output below expected thresholds.

System Disorder - Ch 96

Wound Dehiscence and Evisceration

A11Postop complicationDo not reinsert

Use this when the incision opens or abdominal contents protrude after surgery.

Pathophysiology

  • Dehiscence is wound separation; evisceration is protrusion of internal organs through the incision.
  • Risk rises with obesity, diabetes, poor nutrition, infection, coughing without splinting, hematoma, or steroid use.

Assessment

  • Watch for sudden wound opening, drainage changes, visible tissue, shock signs, pain, or client report that the incision gave way.

Nursing Care

  • Call for help, stay with the client, cover with sterile saline-moistened dressing, bend hips and knees, and notify the provider.
  • Do not push organs back into the wound.

Basic Concept - Ch 96

Postoperative Complication Sorting

A1Clinical judgmentPACU to unit

Use this when a question asks you to match the complication, likely cause, and first nursing response.

Related Content

  • Priority complications include airway obstruction, hypovolemia, paralytic ileus, DVT, and wound dehiscence/evisceration.
  • Risk factors include immobility, anemia, hypovolemia, respiratory disease, cardiac disease, diabetes, malnutrition, obesity, and age-related changes.

Underlying Principles

  • Low urine output, poor skin turgor, rising BUN, tachycardia, or concentrated labs can signal hypovolemia.
  • Absent bowel sounds after abdominal surgery can point toward paralytic ileus.

Nursing Interventions

  • Prioritize ABC changes first, then bleeding/perfusion, wound integrity, bowel function, urine output, and mobility/clot prevention.
  • Encourage ordered ambulation, coughing and deep breathing, splinting, and incentive spirometry while awake.

Basic Concept - Ch 82

Sick-Day Diabetes Safety

A1Unit 12DKA prevention

Use this when illness, poor intake, or GI losses create a hyperglycemia and ketone-monitoring risk.

Related Content

  • Illness can raise glucose even when the client is eating less.
  • Sick-day care protects hydration, glucose control, and early DKA detection.

Underlying Principles

  • Continue prescribed insulin or oral hypoglycemic therapy unless the provider gives a different plan.
  • Check glucose frequently during illness and test urine ketones when glucose is elevated, especially above the source threshold for ketone testing.

Nursing Interventions

  • Teach hydration and carbohydrate-containing fluids when solid food is not tolerated.
  • Escalate for moderate or large ketones, ketonuria lasting more than 24 hr, persistent glucose above 250 mg/dL, fever, confusion, rapid breathing, persistent GI losses, inability to tolerate liquids, or prolonged illness.

Medication - Ch 82

Insulin Timing and Mixing Safety

A7Unit 12Hypoglycemia prevention

Use this when a question asks how to safely time mealtime insulin or whether ordered insulins can be combined.

Purpose

  • Insulin therapy replaces basal and prandial insulin needs to control fasting, between-meal, and postprandial glucose.
  • Rapid-acting insulin is coordinated with meal availability to reduce hypoglycemia risk.

Medication Administration

  • Do not mix glargine or detemir with other insulin; administer in separate injections.
  • When mixing a rapid- or short-acting insulin with a longer-acting insulin that can be mixed, draw up the shorter-acting insulin first.

Nursing Interventions

  • Check glucose before insulin and monitor for sweating, weakness, dizziness, confusion, headache, tachycardia, or slurred speech at peak times.
  • Treat an awake client with hypoglycemia using rapid carbohydrate, then recheck glucose and follow with a longer snack if the next meal is delayed.

Basic Concept - Ch 83

DKA vs HHS Emergency Sorting

A1Unit 12Emergency priorities

Use this when severe hyperglycemia appears and the question asks whether the pattern fits DKA, HHS, or the first treatment priority.

Related Content

  • DKA has hyperglycemia with ketones and metabolic acidosis; HHS has profound hyperglycemia, hyperosmolar dehydration, and little to no ketosis.
  • DKA is closely linked with insufficient insulin; HHS is common in older adults who have type 2 diabetes and dehydration risk.

Underlying Principles

  • Osmotic diuresis causes dehydration and electrolyte shifts in both emergencies.
  • Insulin therapy and acidosis correction can shift potassium into cells, so potassium monitoring is a safety priority.

Nursing Interventions

  • Expect isotonic fluid replacement first, followed by IV regular insulin as prescribed and hourly glucose evaluation.
  • When glucose approaches 250 mg/dL, dextrose-containing fluid is added while treatment continues to reduce hypoglycemia and rapid osmolar-shift risk.
