Worked ExampleTherapeutic CommunicationCh 3 — Effective Communication · Open chapter ↗
Student Name
Concept
Therapeutic Communication
Review Module Chapter
Ch 3 — Effective Communication

Related Content

Purposeful, client-centered communication that builds trust and helps clients express thoughts and feelings.

Techniques: active listening, open-ended questions, silence, reflecting, clarifying, focusing, offering general leads.

Underlying Principles

  • Communication is verbal and nonverbal; observe both
  • Establish trust and an accepting, nonjudgmental environment
  • Empathy keeps the nurse supportive yet objective

Nursing Interventions

  • Use open-ended questions to encourage expression
  • Use silence to allow processing
  • Avoid blocks: advice, "why" questions, false reassurance, changing the subject
  • Reflect feelings and clarify meaning
Worked ExampleDefense MechanismsCh 4 — Stress and Defense Mechanisms · Open chapter ↗
Student Name
Concept
Defense Mechanisms
Review Module Chapter
Ch 4 — Stress and Defense Mechanisms

Related Content

Unconscious mental processes that protect against anxiety. Range from mature/adaptive (altruism, sublimation, suppression) to immature/maladaptive (denial, projection, regression).

Underlying Principles

  • Everyone uses defense mechanisms; they become unhealthy when overused or reality-distorting
  • Repression is unconscious; suppression is conscious
  • Identifying them guides therapeutic intervention

Nursing Interventions

  • Recognize the mechanism without harsh confrontation
  • Help the client develop healthier coping
  • Build insight gradually through the therapeutic relationship

Ethical Principles in MH Nursing
Review Module Chapter
Ch 2 — Legal and Ethical Issues

Related Content

Beneficence (do good), autonomy (self-determination), justice (fairness), fidelity (keep commitments), veracity (truthfulness).

Underlying Principles

  • Clients retain the right to make informed decisions about care
  • Ethical conflicts often weigh autonomy against beneficence/safety

Nursing Interventions

  • Advocate for the client; maintain truthfulness and confidentiality
  • Use the least restrictive interventions

Client Rights and Commitment
Review Module Chapter
Ch 2 — Legal and Ethical Issues

Related Content

Clients keep civil rights during treatment. Voluntary admission: consents, may request discharge. Involuntary commitment: danger to self/others or unable to meet basic needs.

Underlying Principles

  • Right to least restrictive environment, to refuse treatment (with limits), and to confidentiality
  • Involuntary status requires due process and periodic review

Nursing Interventions

  • Document behavior objectively
  • Protect rights; avoid false imprisonment, assault, and battery
  • Follow facility and legal protocols for commitment and restraint

Therapeutic Milieu

Ch 5 — Therapeutic and Safe Environment

Related Content

A deliberately structured, safe environment that promotes healing through clear expectations, consistent limits, and practice of interpersonal skills.

Underlying Principles

  • The environment itself is therapeutic
  • Structure, safety, and consistency reduce anxiety
  • Clients learn through group living and feedback

Nursing Interventions

  • Maintain a safe, low-stimulation, predictable environment
  • Set consistent, clear limits and routines
  • Encourage participation in unit activities and groups

Therapeutic Nurse-Client Relationship
Review Module Chapter
Ch 5 — Therapeutic and Safe Environment

Related Content

A goal-directed, professional relationship progressing through orientation, working, and termination phases. Watch for transference and countertransference.

Underlying Principles

  • Maintain professional boundaries; the relationship serves the client’s needs
  • Transference: client projects feelings onto nurse. Countertransference: nurse projects onto client

  • Recognize and manage transference/countertransference
  • Prepare for termination early to address feelings of loss

Levels of Anxiety
Review Module Chapter
Ch 11 — Anxiety Disorders

Related Content

Mild (heightened learning), moderate (narrowed focus), severe (greatly reduced perception), panic (loss of rational thought, safety risk).

Underlying Principles

  • Mild anxiety can enhance performance; severe/panic impairs functioning
  • Interventions match the level of anxiety

  • Severe/panic: stay with the client, calm low-stimulation environment, simple firm directions; ensure safety

Student Name
Concept
Grief and Loss
Review Module Chapter
Ch 27 — Dying and/or Grieving

Related Content

Normal response to loss. Types include anticipatory, complicated, and disenfranchised grief. Stage theories (Kübler-Ross) and Worden’s Four Tasks of Mourning describe the process.

Underlying Principles

  • Grief is individual and nonlinear
  • Complicated grief is prolonged and functionally impairing
  • Culture and spirituality shape mourning

Nursing Interventions

  • Encourage expression of feelings; use therapeutic presence
  • Respect cultural/spiritual practices
  • Protect against isolation; refer for complicated grief

Student Name
Concept
Crisis and Crisis Intervention
Review Module Chapter
Ch 29 — Crisis Management

Related Content

A crisis occurs when usual coping fails; it is self-limiting (≈4–6 weeks). Types: maturational, situational, adventitious.

Underlying Principles

  • Crisis intervention is short-term and present-focused
  • Goal: return the client to the pre-crisis level of functioning

Nursing Interventions

  • Assess safety first; identify the problem
  • Mobilize support systems and resources
  • Develop concrete, realistic coping steps

Related Content

The body’s physical and psychological response to demands. Coping may be problem-focused or emotion-focused; resilience buffers stress.

Worked ExampleAnxiety DisordersCh 11 — · Open chapter ↗
Student Name
Disorder/Disease Process
Anxiety Disorders
Review Module Chapter
Ch 11 —

Alterations in Health (Diagnosis)

Excessive anxiety/fear that impairs function: GAD, panic disorder, phobias, social anxiety, separation anxiety, agoraphobia.

Pathophysiology Related to Client Problem

Dysregulation of GABA, serotonin, and norepinephrine; heightened autonomic arousal.

Health Promotion and Disease Prevention

Stress management, early treatment of symptoms, limiting caffeine/stimulants.

ASSESSMENT

Risk Factors

  • Family history, female sex, chronic stress/trauma
  • Substance use, certain medical conditions

Expected Findings

  • Restlessness, excessive worry, irritability, sleep disturbance
  • Panic: palpitations, chest pain, dyspnea, sense of doom

Laboratory Tests

No definitive test; labs rule out medical causes (thyroid, cardiac).

Diagnostic Procedures

Clinical interview; standardized screening tools.

SAFETY

Safety Considerations

During panic/severe anxiety, ensure safety and a calm low-stimulation environment.

Complications

Depression, substance use, social/occupational impairment.

PATIENT-CENTERED CARE

Nursing Care

  • Stay with the client during severe anxiety; remain calm
  • Simple clear directions; teach relaxation and cognitive reframing

Medications

SSRIs/SNRIs (first-line), buspirone, short-term benzodiazepines.

Therapeutic Procedures

CBT, exposure/systematic desensitization, relaxation training.

Client Education

  • Medications take weeks for full effect; do not stop abruptly
  • Practice relaxation; limit caffeine

Interprofessional Care

Therapist/psychologist for CBT; provider for medication management.

Neurocognitive Disorders (Delirium & Dementia)
Review Module Chapter
Ch 17 —

Alterations in Health (Diagnosis)

Delirium: acute, fluctuating, reversible. Dementia: gradual, progressive, generally irreversible.

Pathophysiology Related to Client Problem

Delirium from acute medical causes; dementia from neurodegeneration (e.g., Alzheimer plaques/tangles).

Health Promotion and Disease Prevention

See chapter for detail.

ASSESSMENT

Risk Factors

  • Delirium: infection, medications, metabolic disturbance, surgery
  • Dementia: advanced age, vascular disease, genetics

Expected Findings

  • Delirium: acute confusion, altered consciousness, fluctuating course
  • Dementia: progressive memory loss, aphasia, apraxia, agnosia

Laboratory Tests

For delirium, identify the underlying cause (CBC, electrolytes, cultures, drug levels).

Diagnostic Procedures

Cognitive screening (e.g., MMSE); imaging for dementia workup.

SAFETY

Safety Considerations

High fall and wandering risk; protect from injury; reduce environmental hazards.

Complications

Injury, malnutrition, infection, caregiver strain.

PATIENT-CENTERED CARE

Nursing Care

  • Delirium: treat the cause; reorient; calm environment
  • Dementia: consistent routine, simple communication, redirection

Medications

Alzheimer: cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil) and memantine; use antipsychotics cautiously.

Therapeutic Procedures

Cognitive stimulation, structured activities, caregiver support.

Client Education

  • Safety-proof the home; establish routine
  • Caregiver respite and support resources

Student Name
Medication
SSRIs
Category Class
Antidepressant — selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor
Review Module Chapter
Ch 22 —
Purpose of Medication

Expected Pharmacological Action

Block reuptake of serotonin, increasing its availability.

Therapeutic Use

Depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD, panic disorder.

Complications

  • Early: nausea, headache, insomnia
  • Sexual dysfunction; serotonin syndrome

Contraindications/Precautions

Caution with bleeding risk; monitor youth for suicidal ideation.

Interactions

MAOIs (serotonin syndrome — allow 2-week washout); other serotonergic drugs.

Evaluation of Medication Effectiveness

Improved mood/function over several weeks.

Medication Administration

Daily, usually morning; do not stop abruptly (discontinuation syndrome).

Nursing Interventions

  • Monitor mood and suicide risk early in therapy
  • Teach onset takes 4–6 weeks

Client Education

  • Take consistently; report serotonin syndrome signs

Purpose of Medication

Expected Pharmacological Action

  • Fine tremor, GI upset, polyuria, weight gain
  • Toxicity >1.5 mEq/L: coarse tremor, confusion, ataxia, seizures

Contraindications/Precautions

Narrow therapeutic index; caution in renal disease and dehydration.

Interactions

NSAIDs, diuretics, ACE inhibitors raise levels; sodium changes affect levels.

Evaluation of Medication Effectiveness

Stabilized mood; maintenance level 0.6–1.2 mEq/L.

Medication Administration

Take with food; monitor serum levels regularly.

Nursing Interventions

  • Monitor levels and signs of toxicity
  • Ensure consistent sodium and fluid intake

Client Education

  • Maintain steady salt/fluid intake; report toxicity signs
  • Keep lab appointments

Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)
Review Module Chapter

Description of Procedure

A brief electrical stimulus induces a controlled seizure under anesthesia to treat severe psychiatric illness.

Indications

Severe/treatment-resistant depression (esp. with acute suicidality or psychosis), acute mania, some psychoses.

Nursing Interventions (pre, intra, post)

  • Pre: informed consent, NPO, remove dentures/jewelry, baseline vitals; anesthetic, muscle relaxant, atropine given

Improved mood, often after several treatments in a series.

Potential Complications

Temporary memory loss/confusion; risks of anesthesia.

Client Education

  • Expect short-term memory effects that usually resolve
  • Arrange transportation; report concerns
